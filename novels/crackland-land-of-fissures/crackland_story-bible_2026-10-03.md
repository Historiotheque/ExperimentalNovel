# Crackland: Land of Fissures — Story Bible

**Working document · v1.0 · 2026-10-03**
**Status:** living document. Compiled from the source text (the 2024 tentative text of "Crackland & The Crackland Journals," read in full 2026-10-03) and the author's accounts. Purpose: a single, re-feedable record of this novel's world, system, and editorial state. Amend, don't rewrite.

## 1. The work

*Crackland: Land of Fissures* (official title, 2026-10-03) is a single novel, written on the computer in a file called "Crackland & The Crackland Journals" at the same time as *The History-Project* (2000–2004). The two works are essentially fused in one master work: the typewritten *History-Project* manuscript (over 100 pages written, under 100 surviving, much lost to water damage) is the visible part; the Crackland body — about 248,000 words in the 2024 tentative text — is what the project mostly became, and it supplies *The History-Project*'s physical cosmology.

The Preface states the work's own terms: ten years of writing "boiled down to leave only the sediment," a text called a novel because "it is advantageous for me right now to call it one."

## 2. Genesis

From the author's genesis account (April 22, 2004, inside the text): in the summer of 2001 he began a series of paintings on cardboard, "The History Project" — paintings meant to make a viewer think *History* (the first, oil on Masonite, like a computer motherboard with little windows; a grandfather clock for Time; ambiguities and transparencies, because history is often ambiguous and transparent). The painting project became a novel in which the main characters work on "The History Project" — except that theirs is a work of historical therapy, or *historiotherapeusis*: Montreal is suffering from its own history, is being oppressed by it, and the characters are hired to study the city's history and find a solution to its ailment.

Compositional law, stated in the text: no character may be a *writer* first — only painters' journals, doctors' memoirs, historians' letters — because a writer in the text "would destroy the History Project."

## 3. The definition

"The Main Theory of Crackland" (May 22, 2003): Crackland is not about crack cocaine. It is "the Land of Fissures," a "region or domain of fracture and disjunction" — and it is necessary, because without Crackland "the Man of Passages could not exist, for a person of Passages needs… a site or area… which necessitate passage."

Beneath the definition, the deeper axiom: "real things are all crooked and full of fissures, cracked by what necessarily is The Primordial Crack in the Glass: being or existence." The image is born of a fissure, built negatively.

And the approach doctrine: Crackland itself can never be reached — "only a fog balancing over the path-towards which is the real Crackland"; the destination "exists only in premonition."

## 4. Geography (the Geogrammar)

A man-made floating island, "a thousand years old," on the Dread River: a North Shore and a South Shore, Mount-St-Elsewhere, the St-Elsewhere valley, the River of Blood. Both shores are held under a baronial or seigniorial system of land distribution. Anything outside the map is an Elsewhere — and "any movement from one place to another within it is also an Elsewhere."

Sacred geography: St-Elsewhere (the Brotherhood of the Priests of St-Elsewhere; Painter A. "was the Priest of St-Elsewhere" days before becoming Painter A.).

In the trilogy's cosmology chain (series bible, §4), the line of descent passes through Crackland: …Earth → Dream Assembly → St-Elsewhere → Crackland → City-Of templates → … → the Chronotopium. Behind everything, the beginning is broken — "the 'broken record' of Crackland."

## 5. Time

Time in Crackland is plural and folded:

- **Orbikon** = 3.5 of our years, in two cycles: biku (first) and Azlaz (second).
- **Muris** = 3.5 days, in two cycles: Nerik and Malan.
- **The Timefold:** "It appears to us at present that time has folded over the years 1900 and 2000. The fold is at the end of World War II." Other eras (1914, 1907, 1968, 1972) survive in shadows and reflections — Napoleon is sighted in a bathroom.
- **Thin time** (in "Damaged Clockwork"): time broken into action-sized units — toothbrush-time, hallway-walking time — layered so that hours are experienced inside seconds. Against it, "extra-historic time," where a poem or painting floats free of its date.

## 6. The Tonal Cinema system

The governing machine of the work is **Tonal Cinema** (T/I/C — Tonal / Interval / Contact):

- The **Baron** is Projectionist and Projector: "lens mechanism," "Eye of Text," "Sir Iris."
- The **Boy** is Messenger, Light, Medium — he crosses the Interval, carrying messages (including to the dead) for the **Dream Assembly** (the Legion of Souls), whose carriers hold the dreams and realities of the future in their unconscious.
- The **Assembly / City / Cemetery** is "Projector itself or god-machine."

History presents itself through images — *physio-imaging*: "the historical self-presentation of one's history" staged by projection laws. Projection, Interval, Contact, and Possession are the master operations.

The work's space is nested enclosure: "a large square with a smaller square fitted somewhere inside it, and another square inside that one" — Room, House, City — like "two mirrors repeating an image infinitely." The image-generating mechanism "takes on the shape of a Room, a House, a Theatre, or even a City," and works by *block-discharge*: "I excrete blocks of images, images interconnected into functional semi-systematic modules."

## 7. Persons

- **The Baron of St-Hilaire** — "Mr. Change-Face, Sardonopalus, Proteus"; the Watchman; "Man of Passages." A tall man in a green suit and hat, writing at an old Brother typewriter. Present-and-absent power acting through officers: "This is how History works." His house is a memory-machine: a basement archive six times the size of the house, "thousands of cards, detailed with patience and a genius for encryption," retrievable by shaft — an external, material memory built "to unburden himself." His buried document boxes (named "1902" and "1904") wait near a buried raven, to be excavated and deciphered.
- **The Boy (Jean-Pierre)** — messenger and mediator; reads the Baron's epitaph in the village cemetery ("He will make a great Messenger") and is changed by the reading: a transaction occurs the moment the Boy reads the words.
- **Painter A.** — Alexander Gaganov; also Alphonse Lemoyne. The painter as operator: "Cyclicus pictor." (In the Territories schema: Painter A., X in a twelve-zone map of the world — House, Office, Prison, Cemetery, Carnival, City vs. Village, Baron, Boy, Doctor, Painter, Assembly, and the "Pathology of the Meta-brain: Ecstasy of the System.")
- **Edward (Ed) Long** — painter-writer in his studio, forever writing and re-titling a several-hundred-page masterpiece of painting theory. (His name glosses itself in the text: "Edward means 'guardian of wealth'" — beside the treasure/thesaurus complex below.)
- **Victor Vortex** — a Concordia PhD student; books migrate between his shelves and bleed into one another ("the disintegration of books, fiction mixing with factual existence").
- **The Doctors** — Dr. Arthur Kropotkin (writing on "History tout simple"); Dr. Gaganov (projectors, cameras, recordings; three notebooks: I. Meta-Brain Psychosis, II. Physio-Imaging, III. Historiotherapeusis); Dr. Lloyd Emerson, whose career rests on "The Baron Complex."
- **Beatrice** — the booksmith: an NDG bookstore run on a cue-card system indexing 60,000 books; author of a 700-page *Genealogy of Ideas: Cartographies of Lost Time*.
- **E. Varia and Emily** — the "found" letters (Montreal, 1884): 512 letters in a box, edited by a narrator who found them in 2003; Varia's lost book was "a philosophy of history if not a logic of production in the historical arts," dedicated to Emily.
- **Manager X / Minister Xavier** of Type-Co. — the coercive pole: "Coercive forces and Type-Command."

## 8. Master concepts

- **Habit** — the work's master concept, treated axiomatically ("Habit and Having"): "I is a habit." Habits are tendencies; tendencies are values; values are "a directionality"; identity is "a possessed composite of directional tendencies" in a "plastic medium of being." *Habitance*: man's inhabiting of space and temporalizing of time. Technique is habit: "Habits are technical, require to be learned" — house-painting (découpage, rouleau, learned from the author's father) is theorized as the model of all technique.
- **The treasure-chest** — *thesaurus* means both treasury and collection. The closed chest is "anti-temporal" and "anti-historical": objects hidden in it are "stolen from time," preserved outside history — and exhibiting a treasure shortens its veiled life. Locks, keys, and the Circle (the mountain man who walks the circumference forever, never nearer the glowing centre; the Tangent gives temporary escape) belong to this complex.
- **The Labyrinth** — information, concepts, the city. **Alexandria** — library = Babel = Tree of Knowledge: "When Alexandria burns, the secret safe will still exist outside Alexandria."
- **Fossilization** — "Self is created and constituted in fossilization"; "we are mostly made up of ruins, of habits become obsolete. We are fossilized from the interior" (Kropotkin). "History is always in ruins."

## 9. Period terminology → present terminology

The work's own period names for what is now called **historiophany** (named 2026; the recognition, 2026-10-03, that *The History-Project* was always about historiophany):

- **Historiotherapeusis / Historiotherapy** — history as an ailment to be treated: not fixing History but helping one "adjust to its changes," treating traumas that live in customs and language (the French verb *débarquer* still carrying the forefathers' debarking from ships). Also defined as "a.k.a. History-Painting": not depictions of historical events, but paintings where "the painting becomes symptomatic of History and vice versa."
- **Physio-imaging** — history presenting itself as image (see §6).
- **Anaesthesia** — the counter-term: cultural insensibility, "a real historical experience… necessary to history yet blinding."

The manifestation events are already staged as such: the epitaph transaction; history erupting into the present (Feb. 5, 2003, noted in real time: Colin Powell's UN speech delivered while *Guernica* hangs covered); fur-trade voices audible in a present-day park; one Sherbrooke Street holding woodland, trade routes, and 180 years at once.

## 10. Document anatomy

The source text is a fused body, not a sequence: dated journals timestamped to the second (the compositional device that later becomes the logging practice), fiction fragments, letters, treatises ("Habit and Having"; "Essentialism," 2004; the "Process-Painting" treatise, Jan. 2004 — painting as serial "treatments" in a "modal chain"), reading lists and notes, project plans, and an appendix "taken entirely from my own journals." Voices and narrators are deliberately unstable; whole passages repeat word-for-word; whole sections are outlines that never became prose. The text enacts the failure it theorizes — the rant at its centre: the author envisioned "a beautiful, I mean, fucking splendid historical novel the likes of a Dostoevsky… this phenomenological masterpiece 1000+ pages long," and keeps the vision and the impossibility on the same page.

The document does not conclude. It dissolves into notes and ends on a single word: "Achronology."

## 11. Editorial program (open — the author's decisions)

The 2024 tentative text stands as the source of record. An edited edition — up to a near-total rewrite — must decide, in staged batches folded from the Interzone:

1. Order: keep the achronological montage, or re-stratify by the strata (journals / fiction / letters / treatises)?
2. The journals' share: the autobiographical substrate stays visible, or is fully modulated into fiction?
3. The 1884 letters: frame, interleaved current, or appendix?
4. The treatises: in the body, or moved to the theory folder as design concepts?
5. What is cut, and does the cut material live elsewhere in the repo (design concepts? REFMATS?) or only in the archive of record?

No silent rewrites: every batch is staged first, per the repository's standing rules.
