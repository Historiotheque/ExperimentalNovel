# The History-Project

Vol. 1 of *The Revolt of Fiction*.

**Genesis.** Summer 2001: a series of paintings on cardboard — "The History
Project" — meant to paint a concept of history, to make a viewer look and
think *History*. The painting project became a novel. Inside the novel, the
main characters work on "The History Project" themselves, but theirs is a
work of historical therapy — *historiotherapeusis*: Montreal is suffering
from its own history, is being oppressed by it, and they are hired to study
the city's history and find a solution to its ailment. (Genesis account:
April 22, 2004, inside the Crackland text.)

**Form.** Vol. 1 opens the trilogy's disintegration gradient: it begins as
a traditional novel and slowly disintegrates as the in-novel
Archives-Project autopsies it — the examination corrupts the evidence,
retroactively. Inside the fiction, the project failed because it had no
workspace theory: the work couldn't see the room it was happening in.

**The material.** Written on the typewriter, 2001–2002. The manuscript was
over 100 pages; under 100 survive — much was lost to water damage. The bulk
of what the project became lives in its fused companion work,
*Crackland: Land of Fissures* (`../../crackland-land-of-fissures/`), written
on the computer at the same time and supplying this novel's physical
cosmology: the two are essentially one master work. Compositional law,
stated in the text: no character may be a *writer* first — only painters'
journals, doctors' memoirs, historians' letters.

**Working order:** the trilogy is begun with vol. 2, *The Archives-Project*.

**Status:** source materials identified — the surviving typewritten pages
and the Crackland body. Drafting to come, in staged batches.
