# CONSTITUTION

*The doctrine of the incorruptible source.*

## Preamble

The source must never be corrupted — not by haste, not by experiment, not by the operator's own errors. What is released must stay released, exactly as it was. This is how.

## Article 1 — main is sacred

`main` is always releasable, always clean. No experiment is ever committed to `main` directly. `main` is the diamond: palatial, crystalline, incorruptible — the NOUS of the repository.

## Article 2 — branches are workshops

All work happens on branches: one branch per unit of work, branched from `main`, merged back, deleted. `feature/...`, `fix/...` — small, named, disposable. Branches are the contained workshop where experiments and failures happen safely. The Invisible Forces get the workshop; they never touch the crystal.

## Article 3 — merges are the Archivillus moment

Merge with `--no-ff`, so every branch stays visible in the history. The history must read like a lab notebook: every merge a small dated story of what was tried, what failed, what was kept. To merge is to collect, log, amend, and fold in.

## Article 4 — versions are immutable

Tag every release (`v1.0`, `v1.1`). A tag is an immutable pointer: the released version can never silently change under anyone. "The latest version" always resolves to a tag, never to a moving target.

## Article 5 — review, even alone

Open a pull request for every merge and review the diff fresh — ideally a day later. The solo dev reviews themselves: adversarial review against one's own operator errors, before they reach the diamond.

## Article 6 — the gate, not the magic

Incorruptibility is not magic. It is a gate: nothing reaches `main` except through a branch, a review, and a merge. History is append-only and content-addressed — tamper-evident, walk-backable. What inspires is the visible discipline: a public history clean enough to read.
