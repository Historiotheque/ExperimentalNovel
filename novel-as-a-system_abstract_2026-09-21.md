# The Novel as a System — an abstract of a novelistic philosophy

*Draft abstract — 2026-09-21. For The Experimental Novel.*

## 1. Not Zola's experiment

The "experimental novel" proposed here owes nothing to Zola's *Le Roman expérimental* (1880). Zola's experiment was deterministic: the novel as a laboratory demonstrating heredity and milieu, the writer as a clinician observing characters the way a physiologist observes bodies. The experiment proposed here is *generative and systems-based*: the novel reconceived for the 21st century as a machine for producing texts, meanings, and states of attention — an apparatus the writer builds, operates, and is in turn operated by. Where Zola wanted the novel to prove laws, this practice wants the novel to *enact systems*: feedback loops, emergences, collapses, recombinations. The novel is not the report of an experiment. The novel *is* the experiment, still running.

## 2. The novel-as-a-system

A novel-as-a-system is a novel understood as an organized totality of interacting parts — conceptual scheme, cast, archive, procedures, and output formats — that generates more than the sum of its components. Every novel in this practice carries its own conceptual scheme (its private metaphysics, its rules of motion, its theory of what a sentence may do); the scheme is not decoration applied afterward but the *operating system* on which the fiction runs. *The Nihilist* is the clearest instance to date: the same Athens episode compiled twice, once as narrative (Part I) and once as executable program (Part II) — story and source code as mirror images, the telling and the compiling as the two hands of a single work. The system is the work; any given chapter is one of its possible executions.

This conception has deep roots in the practice: a dozen years of Python as a daily instrument; the conception of the studio itself as cultural software, versioned and released; the Database Arts, in which works exist as addressable entries in a vast unordered set; Schizobot, the ironic machine built to replace the artist. The novel-as-a-system is the literary branch of a single conviction: that artworks in this century are best understood — and best built — as *systems that produce aesthetic events*, not as objects that merely are.

## 3. Novelistic phenomenologies

If the novel-as-a-system is the *apparatus*, novelistic phenomenology is its *method of knowing*. A novelistic phenomenology is a work of fiction written as an instrument for investigating the structures of experience: what it is like for consciousness to encounter time, death, meaning, absurdity, revelation — not described from outside, but *enacted* from inside, in the only laboratory where experience can be staged without being destroyed, which is the sentence. The novel becomes a witness-box: a constructed chamber in which a mode of being can testify to itself. (The Inner Historiotheque — the primordial witness-box of existence from which all the work stems — is the private name of this chamber.)

This is why the practice keeps returning to extreme or limit-figures — the witness who cannot not exist, the archivist of collapsing worlds: limit-figures are phenomenological probes. Placed at the boundary of the thinkable, they report back. The cut-up, the Markov chain, the constraint, the erasure are not gimmicks but *reductions* in the phenomenological sense: techniques for suspending habit so that the structure of meaning-making shows itself. Chance operations reveal what intention conceals; decay reveals what preservation hides.

## 4. The completable work

A consequence of the systems view: these novels are not finished, they are *completable*. A novel-as-a-system defines a space of possible instantiations rather than a fixed sequence of chapters; new episodes, voices, and executions can be added for as long as the system runs — which is to say, for as long as the writer (or a future operator, human or otherwise) keeps feeding it. The work is indefinite without being formless: the conceptual scheme constrains what counts as a valid move, the way the rules of a game constrain without predicting play. Posterity is therefore not an audience but a potential *co-operator* of the system.

## 5. The stakes

Underneath the machinery lies a single wager: that fiction is a legitimate organ of research — that there are truths about being, time, and meaning that can only be reached by building the machine that enacts them and then watching what it does. The experimental novel, in this sense, is continuous with the rest of the practice (the paintings, the field recordings, the refcards, the archives): one more instrument, tuned to the frequency of narrative, for plumbing the depths. *Contemplating the mysteries* — by system, by procedure, by sentence.
