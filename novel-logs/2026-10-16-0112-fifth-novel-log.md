## What I did

**The novel arc, settled (2026-10-11).** Post-archival, in my plain
meaning, is *post-archive*: the reality after the collapse of my
archives. My archive strategy under The History-Project and The
Archives-Project failed, as I judge it, and the dismantling was my own
— the ironic twist. The story of that failure will be told in the
novels themselves: at the end of *The Archives-Project* and the
beginning of *The Chronotopium*, the novel where the Archivillus
system is built. The archive failure is narrative material,
deliberately kept.

**Abstract Characterology: restated, sourced, consolidated
(2026-10-14 → 2026-10-16).** I gave the work its own channel and
restated the core: a fictional character is a character-problem, and
the problem is what defines the character; what defines a person is
their existential knot — a problem, or a series / sequence of
problems. Then I folded in my own earlier sources — the summer 2026
Antilog posts and excerpts from my conversations with a collaborator —
and the fold-in recovered formulations I had already made, and added
new ones:

- **P-Space generates S-Space** (my formulation, 2026-07-15): defining
  each character's problem space, which is connected with a solution
  space in that the P-Space generates the S-Space. The novel is a
  problematique; a character has, and is, a problematique — naked
  problems, walking, talking problems.
- **The character as hypothesis:** the character-as-problem is also the
  character-as-hypothesis, making a testable and reproducible
  prediction about reality — its own reality as a fictional object and
  device in the novel.
- **Characterological conflict** — my new term for the core: a deep,
  ongoing psychological state where a person's ingrained behavioral
  patterns and personality traits clash directly with their own core
  values; the character has a blueprint, a personal constitution, and
  constantly goes against it. Its historical-scale form is **value
  transgressions**: anything that puts the Cultural Treasure in danger,
  or its faithful transcription.
- **Premise-is-the-problem:** a novel's premise is expressible in a
  one-liner, and the premise is the problem. Mine: *The History-Project*
  (the project): "Is it possible to paint the concepts of history in a
  way people would understand?" *The History-Project* (the novel): "An
  interdisciplinary team tries to figure out why the Greater Montreal
  Region historically has a higher incidence of anxiety and depression
  than the rest of Canada." *The Archives-Project*: "The same
  interdisciplinary team as in The History-Project investigates the
  failure of The History-Project, what the risk factors were, how it
  occurred exactly, by looking at the archives."
- The theory's style-ancestor, in my framing: a characterology in the
  style of Reich's, except abstract; its ancestor in my own work, the
  *Esquisses caractérielles* I conceived in 2007. The same basic
  characters keep coming back around, keep haunting my consciousness.
- **Believability** is a required topic of the theory: the characters
  have to behave as real people would. My method is observation — of
  people, and of myself, the human being I have studied the most.
- The theory's **in-fiction provenance**: inside *The Archives-Project*,
  the Author hires a script supervisor to ensure continuity, and it is
  the script supervisor who comes up with the book "Abstract
  Characterology," at first to keep track of all the characters. He is
  not good at his job, so there are constant discontinuities — and the
  narrator is also unreliable. Across the trilogy the characters grow
  more and more algorithmic — **algorithmic being**: character-agents
  who collaborate in writing the novel, each with a Manual of protocols
  for the kind of fiction I write.

**The in-fiction structural doctrine (2026-10-15).** The fictional
History-Project and Archives-Project both *failed* because they did
not have the Workspace Theory — Delta-Workspace Theory did not exist
yet. It is discovered between *The Archives-Project* and *The
Chronotopium*, and it is the basis and foundation on which the
Archivillus System is built: workspace mechanics as the core engine
inside Archivillus; friction, noise, and transcription errors treated
as necessary dynamic inputs, not destructive flaws — friction as fuel;
Chronoplasticity. Behind the crises stand the Invisible Forces, the
cause of every fissure, fracture, and failure, come out of NOUS — and
MAUD, the Mother of Dreams. And what the novels finally are, in my
formulation: they are not novels — I believe I created a new form.
They exist in the mind as simulations: **novelistic phenomenologies**.
The seed document of the whole theory, DC-2026-001, "The
Novel-as-a-System" (2026-06-21), was located in the Library during
this work; its Characterology passage is where this line starts.

**Character-Space, ruled finite (2026-10-16).** My coinage:
Character-Space, the space of all possible character-agents /
character-problems. My ruling: it is **finite** — a vast and great
number of possibilities, never an infinity — by the same argument as
my Picture Space essay, *The Realm of Finite Possibilities* (© 2016):
a 256×256 bitonal field already holds 2^65536 configurations; a space
built from finite components has a finite, if astronomical,
population. Possible, realizable, and actually realized stay distinct.
In the notation: U := Character-Space, |U| finite.

**Functional Style: the five modes adopted — scoped (2026-10-16).**
Sequence, Branch, Merge, Feedback, Interleave — proposed to me on
2026-10-08 and carried unadopted since — I adopted today, *not* for
the entirety of the art-research practice, but for the specific places
where they apply most elegantly. Elegance is the scope limiter. The
formalization (Version 0.1.0, Draft) restates the primitives from
Procedural Theory v1.0.0 — which it does not amend — and proposes the
modes mapped onto the novels: Sequence as a character's trajectory
(and the page-a-day as its discipline); Branch as the exploration of
Character-Space, and the cast as my own knot refracted across
personae; Merge as knots composed — the city of *The History-Project*,
whose knot is its history, and the Timefold — with the Crackland
composition law (no character may be a *writer* first) read as a
Merge-prohibition, preventing the one merge that would collapse the
frame; Feedback as habit ("I is a habit" — the fixpoint of an
attempted solution iterated), as SOLVE generating the next problem, as
metafictional awareness, and as the Trial of the Author; Interleave as
polyphony, and as the formal shape of the pull of others — Sartre's
pull, in my reading, is what interleaving does to a chain, and shame
an alien selection arriving mid-composition. Achronology, on this
reading, is the honest terminal state of such a composition: it stops;
it does not conclude. The formalization routes five decision points
to this channel (§Decisions, open).

## Ideas and sketches

- **Story as composition (proposal, from the formalization §7):** a
  novel is a composition of character-functions over a shared
  world-state — the world poses the premise-problem, each character's
  SOLVE steps transform both the character and the world, scenes are
  checkpointed applications, the plot is the interleaved trace, and
  the story is the elegant solution whose existence the finished
  composition proves by construction.
- **Historiomics in the same grammar (proposal):** a generation's
  trajectories — interleaved, judged, error-corrected, handed on — and
  the story of the archives' failure, told where the Archivillus
  system is built, is that composition narrated from inside.
- **Pattern Language tap (noted, not filed):** "adopt a formalism
  only where it applies most elegantly — elegance as the scope limiter
  of an adoption" is a candidate pattern for *A Pattern Language for
  Art Operations* — proposed here, filed nowhere yet.
